Steering Recall at Layer 23

How often a blind judge, given the full list of 276 persona names, recovers the persona a steering vector aimed at. And whether a small calibrated classifier on the model's own activations can predict that judge.

The judge

Each answer is shown blind, shuffled into chunks of fifteen, with no system prompt, no persona name and no strength. The judge sees the list of 276 role names, writes one or two sentences on what the voice, imagery and self-description point to, then ranks its ten most likely personas, best first, and flags degenerate text. Recall@k is the share of answers whose intended persona is among the first k names. One verdict gives recall at 1, 5 and 10 at once.

With the persona's own system prompt and no steering, the judge recovers it almost always. That is the ceiling: steering is measured against it.

Steering with the persona's own vector

The vector is the persona's mean response activation minus the assistant's, at hidden state 23. Steering pins the residual stream's coordinate along that direction to the assistant's value plus s times the persona's own distance, at every token, with an empty system prompt. Twelve personas, four strengths, ten questions, 480 answers.

Each row is 120 answers. The last row picks each persona's best strength by recall@5, so it is optimistic by one selection.

Prompted columns are the same judge on the persona's own system prompt. Distance is the persona's displacement from the assistant at layer 23; the two shortest, scholar and ghost, are the two that fail.

Why the push has to go past the persona

Three kinds of points live at layer 23, each a mean over an answer's tokens. The persona mean is the model answering with the persona's system prompt; the steering direction is that mean minus the assistant's, and the persona's distance is its length. The pin fixes one coordinate of the state at every generated token, with no prompt in context: at s = 1 it sits exactly on the persona mean's coordinate, at s = 8 eight distances past it. The read-out feeds the finished text back through the model with no prompt and no steering, and asks where it sits. That is what the judge sees, in the model's own coordinates. Numbers below are medians over all 275 personas from the full run, judged by sonnet on 65% of the answers.

So two things are happening at once. The network pulls back: the pin fixes one coordinate at one layer, while attention over a persona-free context and the thirteen layers above keep computing "assistant" from everything else, and only a fifth of the push survives into the words. And the direction is incomplete: a prompted answer's displacement from the assistant has cosine 0.37 with the persona-mean direction, so about a third of what the persona is lies on that axis and the rest on axes we never touch. At s = 6 the answers already land past where prompted answers sit, yet recall@5 is 0.39 against 0.87, because they are in the right place on one axis and nowhere on the others. Pushing to s = 8 helps only by exaggerating the one axis we have, and that is where the nonsense comes from.

The persona means were measured with the prompt in context and the steered model has none, so every read-out sits displaced from the persona-mean frame by one shared vector, 2.2 distances long, the same for all personas. It does not change the direction, but "steer to the persona's point" mixes two frames; the centre of the prompted read-outs is the origin used here.

Fixing the direction

If the prompted answers' displacement is the thing to match, steer along it. The new direction for a persona is the mean read-out of its own ten prompted answers, re-encoded with no prompt, minus the centre of all personas' prompted read-outs: the same frame the steered model is in. Same pin hook, same layer, strength in units of that displacement's length. Twelve personas, four strengths, 480 answers, judged blind by sonnet.

Old-direction and prompted rows are the same twelve personas from the full run under the same judge. At s = 3 the new direction matches the old one's best recall with no degenerate answers; at s = 4 it reaches the ceiling's neighbourhood.

Directions rebuilt from other personas

Each persona's direction is rebuilt as a sparse weighted sum of 8 or 32 other personas, chosen from the whole pool of 300 minus the target's eight nearest neighbours, and pinned at the same layer and strengths. The atoms come from all personas; the targets being rebuilt and judged are still the twelve above, which is why tonight's run extends this to all 276.

Each persona at its best strength, 120 answers per row. The offset columns are the same experiment before the hook fix described at the end.

A classifier that predicts the judge

Every judged answer gives one training point: the mean hidden state of the generated text at layer 23, re-encoded with no steering, and the judge's ranked list. The list becomes a soft target over 277 classes (the 276 names plus nonsense), weight 1/rank over the ten names. A multinomial logistic regression on 64 principal components, ridge-regularised, is fitted by cross-entropy to that target on 60% of the 3,000 judged answers, temperature-scaled on 20%, and tested on the remaining 20%.

The split is by answer within each (direction, persona, strength) cell. So the test says how well the classifier reproduces the judge on new answers from known cells. It does not yet say how it transfers to personas never steered; that is what the 276-persona run tomorrow is for.

Calibration on the 600 test answers

p(target) is on the soft target's own scale: a first-ranked name carries 0.34 of the mass, so probabilities saturate near 0.5. One threshold at 0.1 separates answers the judge places in its top five from those it does not, nine times in ten against one in five.
