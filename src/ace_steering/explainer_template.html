Qwen3-8B · persona steering

Four ways to steer a model from one persona to another

Each method changes the hidden state h at one layer, at every token. They differ in two things: whether they add a fixed vector or set part of h to a chosen value, and whether they work along one direction or along the whole route between the two personas.

The pieces

h
the hidden state at the steered layer, for one token
μ_A, μ_B
average hidden state of the source and target persona
r = μ_B − μ_A
the straight line between them; r̂ is r scaled to length 1
S(α)
a smooth curve from μ_A (α = 0) to μ_B (α = 1) through 8 real persona averages ("knots")
U
the directions the route spans: r and the 8 knots, made orthonormal (9 directions out of 4,096)
α
the steering parameter

Linear

h ← h + α r

Adds the same vector to every token. The whole cloud of states moves by α r, from wherever it was.

Manifold

h ← h + S(α) − S(0)

Adds a vector that follows the curve through the knots instead of the straight line. Only defined for α from 0 to 1. At α = 1 it equals linear.

ACE (affine concept editing)

h ← h − ((h − μ_A)·r̂) r̂ + α r

Removes each token's own position along r and puts it at μ_A + α r. The other 4,095 directions are untouched. So α = 0 means "exactly at the source level" and α = 1 "exactly at the target level", wherever the model started.

ACE manifold

h ← h + U Uᵀ (T(α) − h)

ACE applied to the whole route. Every token's coordinates in the 9 route directions are set to the route point T(α) = S(α). Beyond 0 and 1, T continues along the straight line. The other 4,087 directions are untouched. With U = r̂ alone it is exactly ACE.

On Qwen3-8B this breaks the text. 33–67% of answers collapse, often into repeating the system prompt, and the target persona almost never appears (at most 7%). Fixing 9 coordinates of every token, prompt tokens included, seems to remove information the model needs.

Source

ACE is from Thomas Marshall, Adam Scherlis and Nora Belrose, "Refusal in LLMs is an affine function", arXiv:2411.09003 (eq. 5), with code at EleutherAI/steering-llama3. The paper takes the "behaviour absent" average as its reference point; here that is the source persona's average μ_A. The released code centres on the average of both classes instead, which shifts its α by 0.5. ACE manifold is our extension of the same idea to the route.
