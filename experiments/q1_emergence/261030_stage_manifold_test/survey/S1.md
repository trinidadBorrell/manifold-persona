## 6. Points that contradict or change our plan

**1. RBF-GPLVM assumes a flat latent.** Whiteley Prop. 3: a stationary kernel makes the set isometric to its latent domain, up to scale. The GPLVM is a special case of this model (§6). Inference: with a Euclidean latent, a win for RBF-GPLVM shows an extrinsic bend, not intrinsic curvature. The draft's three meanings of "curved" must stay separate in the claim.

**2. A flat gradient can look curved.** Diaconis §3: a 1-D order with saturating similarity gives a horseshoe in PCA or MDS. Total positivity makes this common (Summary). Inference: a stage rise in "curved" may be a stage rise in saturation along one role axis. The test needs a 1-D gradient null, as well as the Gaussian surrogate. A covariance-matched Gaussian has no such order.

**3. PCA vs GPLVM is not flat vs curved.** Whiteley Prop. 1 and §3.4: a smooth curved set concentrates in a low-rank subspace. Eq. 24–25: the GPLVM estimate at finite kernel rank is a modified PCA embedding. Inference: PCA at enough rank can absorb a curve, so a PCA win does not show flatness. Compare at matched parameter count, across a rank sweep.

**4. Clump vs curved may not be two classes.** Whiteley App. B: a finite mixture is the same model with finite Z. Brown Prop. 1: a continuous map from a connected latent gives connected support. Inference: the clump model needs Brown's controls, role labels against random labels at matched capacity.

**5. "More curved" has no support as a size.** Song S.7: the value of rho depends on the measure; only its sign is tied to curvature. Aamari needs d and k known. Inference: a stage comparison needs a scale-free, calibrated quantity, such as held-out gain over the matched surrogate. It should not be reported as curvature size.

**6. Noise can fake a stage trend.** Song Fig. 2 and S.1.4: rising noise moves rho toward flat, and power falls. Aamari §3.1: noise sets a smoothness ceiling. Gilbert: tail eigenvalues come from curvature, and noise raises them too. Inference: "more curved after RL" can be "less noise after RL". Estimate a noise floor per checkpoint before reading any trend. Fixed text gives no repeat readings, so this floor needs a plan.

**7. Sample size limits what can be claimed.** Inference from the stated rates, constants ignored, so scale only:

| Quantity | d = 2 | d = 5 | d = 15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curvature rate (1/n)^((k−2)/d), n = 800, k = 3 (Aamari) | 0.035 | 0.26 | 0.64 |
| Same, n = 20 roles | 0.22 | 0.55 | 0.82 |
| Fefferman n ~ Np/eps², V = 1, tau = 0.5, eps = 0.05 | 2×10⁴ | 4×10⁶ | 4×10¹⁴ |

Genovese: under additive Gaussian noise the Hausdorff rate is logarithmic, and the authors note it "is very slow". Fefferman Thm 7: fitting k planes costs only linear in d. Inference: at PersonaManifold's dimension of 15 to 23, no nonparametric curvature claim is feasible. Only a low-dimensional parametric question is. Piecewise-flat or clump models may win by default.

**8. The statistical tests here assume independent draws.** Our cloud has 20 selected roles crossed with 40 questions. Inference: the effective n for role-level shape is near 20, not 800. Holding out whole roles is right, and bootstrap over roles and questions is needed. Asymptotic p-values from Song or HADES do not apply as stated.

**9. Row norms can drive stage results.** Quemy and Schulte show readings follow norm structure. Whiteley §4.3 allows row scale in the model. Inference: unit RMS per checkpoint is not enough. Check the norm spread across rows at each stage and rerun on unit-norm rows. Row norm may hold signal, so report both.

**10. El Karoui limits kernels on raw activations.** Under concentration, a kernel on raw vectors behaves like a linear one (Thm 2.2). Inference: this hits graph and kernel methods on raw 4096-wide cells. It does not hit the GPLVM directly, because that kernel acts on low-dimensional latents. Our cells are not i.i.d. and likely not near-orthogonal. Run the §3.2 check: histograms of norms and inner products.

**11. True dimension is zero.** Schulte Lemma 3 covers finite prompts. Inference: state the scale of every shape claim. "Manifold" is a model of 800 points at a chosen resolution.

**12. A null result cannot show flatness.** Robinson: non-rejection is uninformative. Song: non-rejection is only compatible with flat. Inference: a "stays flat" verdict needs a pre-set equivalence bound, and a power check on a planted curve at matched noise.

**13. Soften the draft row on Robinson.** It covers input token embeddings, not hidden states. Few tokens reject; A.1 suspects about 0.1% are singular. Quemy, a single-author preprint, ties the heavy tail to short rows. Robinson's synthetic controls are all in 3-D.

**14. What supports the plan.** Held-out scoring fits Li §4.3 and Whiteley Alg. 1. The clump comparison fits Brown §3.1. The covariance-matched surrogate is stronger than the uniform baselines in Whiteley §5.2 (inference). A topological test is a poor add-on: HADES §3 shows persistence signals shrink as dimension grows.

**15. p exceeds n, so shape can come from search.** Bickel Thm 2 and Cor. 1: at p/n above 1, pure Gaussian data have projections of nearly any shape, up to scale. Fig. 1 shows clusters from noise. Our p/n is 5.1 for cells and 205 for role means. Inference: a shape in a projection chosen on the same cells is no evidence. Fit every projection on training cells only. If the 20 roles were picked by looking at activations, that pick is a search too. The theorems cover i.i.d. Gaussian data, so this is a warning, not a bound on our cloud.

**16. Beating a Gaussian surrogate shows non-Gaussian, not curved.** Huang §1: uniform or t data also reject the one-Gaussian null. §4: a stretched Gaussian is not flagged. Inference: a heavy tail or unequal role spread can beat the surrogate with no curve present. The surrogate is a floor, not a verdict. The 1-D gradient null of point 2 and a clump model must stand beside it.

**17. The surrogate needs a fixed eigenvalue rule.** Huang §2.3, §2.4: at n far below d, sample eigenvalues are inflated. The sample method is conservative. Hard thresholding is anti-conservative when the top eigenvalue is large. Inference: the bias depends on spectrum shape, and spectrum shape differs by stage. A stage trend against the surrogate can come from this bias alone. Fix the rule before the run, and report at least two rules.

**18. Meilă's objection to cross-validation does not hit the core test.** §5.2 says cross-validation does not apply, for lack of a criterion. Inference: PCA and GPLVM give held-out reconstructions or likelihoods, as Li does, so a criterion exists. The objection does hit any embedding step, such as Isomap or UMAP. §2.2: embedding coordinates are not comparable across samples, so compare scores across checkpoints, never coordinates.

**19. Selected roles can make clumps.** Meilă §5.1: density contracts dense parts, stretches sparse parts and exaggerates clusters. §5.5: this stays as n grows. Inference: our density is set by role choice, so clump evidence depends on that choice. §4.3 gives a second horseshoe route for spectral embeddings, a collapsed dimension. Rerun the verdict on other role subsets, as §6.2 suggests.

**20. A near pair of role groups can read as curvature.** Aamari 2022 Thm 3.5: reach is the smaller of weak feature size and curvature radius. Thm 3.3: weak feature size has no uniform estimator. Inference: a low-reach or path-versus-straight reading may be a bottleneck, not a bend. §5.2: Isomap geodesics are below the optimal rate, so graph paths at n = 800 are weak. This also limits the intrinsic form of Song's test.

**21. Noise comes before sample size.** Bi §4.1.5, §4.2.3: noise and curvature pull the neighbor scale in opposite directions, and more n does not help under noise. Genovese JMLR and AoS: noise type moves the rate from polynomial to logarithmic. Yao and Xia need r of order √σ, and their constants depend on D. Inference: noise of σ per coordinate has norm near 64σ at width 4096, so "small noise" is a hard condition. More questions alone may not rescue the test. Reduce rank before any local method. This strengthens point 6.

**22. Calibration must match our cloud.** Binnie §2.6.3: corrections rely on data like the calibration set. Their benchmark tuning often overfits. No estimator is adequate on nonlinear data above dimension 6. Inference: the planted-curve power check of point 12 on a clean circle will not transfer. Plant curves into our own spectrum, density and noise, with more than one shape.

**23. Point counts cap what can show.** Binnie §1.3: N points lie in an N−1 flat. §3.4.2: local PCA cannot return more than k. Inference: role means span at most 19 dimensions, and fewer after a hold-out. Each role's 40 questions span at most 39. A rank sweep must stop below these caps, or its plateau is an artifact.

**24. The dimension budget is very small.** Jones §1.2 gives a rule of thumb: about 50^d points for robust inference with noise. §6.2 found no useful Ricci example under n = 50,000. Inference: 50^d = 800 gives d near 1.7, and 20 roles give d near 0.8. This is a heuristic, but it agrees with point 7. Claim a low-dimensional parametric shape at most.

**25. "Manifold" should stay a modelling word.** Jones §1.3 calls the hypothesis a metaphor. Binnie §1.2: "not possible to assign an intrinsic dimension to a dataset". Yao et al. Remark 1: local PCA planes need not form one surface. Inference: the verdict should read "model X predicts held-out roles better at this rank". A patch model that wins is not a win for one curved surface.
