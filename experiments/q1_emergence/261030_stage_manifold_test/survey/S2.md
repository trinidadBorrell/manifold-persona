| S8 Euclidean metric | Rank order only, under unknown monotone maps. Metric must be invariant to unit permutation. Per-channel scale changes estimates. | 8, 16, 9 |

## 6. Points that contradict or change the plan

### Largest changes

**1. Flat, clump and curved do not cover the options.** Paper 15 states that summed multilinear components are "no longer confined to a linear subspace despite still being a multilinear method". Paper 11 shows product tuning gives a tensor-product covariance with high linear dimension. Paper 10 shows a distorted cuboid is high dimensional and still keeps its factors.

Inference: in a role × question cloud, a GPLVM win over PCA can come from a low-rank interaction or a gain effect. No smooth curve is needed. Add a fourth rival: additive role and question means plus a low-rank interaction term.

**2. A Gaussian that matches only unit covariance is too weak.** This changes the `LITERATURE.md` item "covariance-matched Gaussian surrogate". Paper 1 shows surrogates that keep fewer modes gave significance where the full surrogate did not (stimulus readout, Fig 4f). Paper 1 also shows standard shuffles misstate confidence because they destroy tuning.

Inference: a unit-only Gaussian draws 800 independent cells. It removes roles and questions, so almost any model beats it. Keep role and question structure in the null.

**3. TME does not keep role centroids directly.** Paper 1 Supp Note 1 defines the first-moment constraint per mode. Here that is 20 role scalars, 40 question scalars and 4096 unit means. It is not the 20 × 4096 role mean matrix. The covariance has a fixed Kronecker-sum form (Supp Note 3).

Inference: use a two-step crossed null.
- Remove the grand mean, then split each cell by ANOVA as in paper 7: role part (19 df), question part (39 df), interaction (741 df).
- Keep the role and question parts exact. Replace only the interaction with a surrogate that keeps its role, question and unit covariances.
- Report a ladder as paper 1 does: unit only, unit plus role, unit plus question, all three.
- Add wording and replicate levels below the cell (see point 4).

No paper in this strand does this. It needs a simulation check first.

**4. Use the repeats to split interaction from noise.** Our data has repeats: each role × question cell holds 5 wordings × 5 replicates. So the "one value per cell" problem does not apply. Paper 7 gets its noise term from trials, and its signal variance needs repeats. Paper 10 trains on trial-level noise clouds. Paper 12 SI states noise is "indistinguishable from a high-dimensional manifold" without further information. Paper 20 gets an unbiased signal spectrum from two repeats.

Inference: the 741 interaction df can now be tested against a within-cell error term.
- The design is nested: cell, then wording, then replicate. It gives two noise levels, not one.
- Replicates of one wording estimate sampling noise. Variation across wordings estimates text-specific variation.
- Replicates share a wording, so they are not 25 independent draws. Treat wording as the unit when the claim must survive a text change.
- Any split for cross-validation must keep this nesting. Split wordings across folds for the main test, and split replicates only for the sampling-noise check.
- Averaging 25 repeats lowers noise but does not add cells. Shape is still estimated on 800 cell means.

The second text source (E3 `ex2`) in `LITERATURE.md` is no longer needed to get repeats. It still matters for text generality, which 5 wordings of one text base only partly test.

**5. "More curved" may mean "more of the same curve".** Paper 4 states flat fits hold locally, and that nonlinearity shows as more surface is sampled. It found this across tasks and condition counts. Paper 11 states observed linear dimension is limited by the values sampled. Paper 18 states a plane fits well when a task explores a small region.

Inference: if SFT spreads the 20 roles apart, the score can rise with no change in shape. Unit RMS rescaling does not remove this, because curvature × extent has no units. The claim should be "the cloud departs more from flat". Also plot the margin against the number of roles (for example 5, 10, 15, 20) at each checkpoint, as paper 4 Fig S4 does for conditions.

**6. Sharper role tuning alone can raise the score.** Paper 11 states linear dimension grows as tuning narrows, with intrinsic dimension unchanged. Paper 12 SI states narrower tuning gives a more convoluted manifold.

Inference: a stage effect may be selectivity, not a new geometry. This is still a real change, but it should be named as such. Measure the sparsity of role effects per checkpoint.

### Limits on what can be claimed

**7. Do not report a curvature value.** Papers 12 and 16 validate shape against a measured variable, and paper 16 needs the topology first. Paper 16 notes curvature scales with inverse radius, so a size change moves the number. Paper 5 gives curvature numbers with no interval or null.

Inference: with no role coordinate, report model margins, not a curvature number. PersonaManifold's Ollivier–Ricci values have the same gap.

**8. Rejecting a null does not show curvature.** Paper 1 states rejection rules out a simpler explanation and does not prove the named structure. It also states failure to reject may mean the data are inadequate.

Inference: state both directions in `THRESHOLDS.md` before the run.

**9. Do not use intrinsic dimension estimators here.** Paper 9 states all methods failed above about 20 or with little data. Correlation Dimension needs samples of order 10^(d/2). Paper 9 also found nonlinear estimators underestimate above about 10.

Inference: 800 cells cannot support d near PersonaManifold's 15–23.

**10. Clump and curve are not exclusive.** Paper 5 fits a mixture whose components are each curved. Paper 2 allows several manifolds.

Inference: add a clumps-with-local-curve outcome to the decision table, or state that the test cannot name it. Cumulants alone will not settle this, since mixtures are also non-Gaussian.

**11. Evidence for curved neural manifolds is weaker than the headline.** Paper 4 has no surrogate null for its index. Its reconstruction error uses a different distance target per method (Methods). A linear-unit RNN also gave a nonlinear result (Fig S8A). Paper 9 calls a 6–8 point VAF gain modest (S1 Table legend).

Inference: treat these as motives, not as a validated method. Score flat and curved models on the same held-out target.

### New from papers 17–25

**12. Held-out success does not protect a curved fit.** Paper 22 fitted neural data to arbitrary curves by linear regression. The fit carried over to held-out conditions, because responses were alike across conditions. Paper 17 states a fitted trajectory may reflect the model as much as the data.

Inference: this limits the `LITERATURE.md` hold-out rule. Keep whole-role hold-out, but add a target that should fail. For example, fit the same model after permuting role labels on the latent side, and report the projected data beside the GPLVM fit.

**13. Use the repeats to clean the spectrum first.** Paper 20's cvPCA takes directions from one repeat and scores them on another. Only reliable variance survives. It recovered a known spectrum in noise simulations where plain PCA did not. A control with 32 images saturated at 32 dimensions.

Inference: run cvPCA per checkpoint, across split wordings. This sets the flat rival's rank from reliable variance. Paper 20 needs noise that is i.i.d. across repeats, so wordings are only an approximation (N17).

**14. The clump control needs its own null.** Paper 24 compares the best k-means silhouette with draws from one Gaussian of matched mean and covariance. It states that sorted plots show apparent clusters even in null data. Paper 21 adds a positive control with synthetic clusters at real noise levels. Paper 24 tests units, not conditions.

Inference: apply both to cell or role points. This gives a clump test that does not depend on any curved model. Moving it from units to conditions is my step and needs a simulation check.

**15. Count distinct roles before reading shape.** Paper 24 states the number of independent conditions caps PCA dimension. That number ranged from 5 to 16 of 16 across regions, and rose along the hierarchy.

Inference: a stage effect may mean that more roles became distinct, with no change in shape. Report pairwise role decodability per checkpoint. This is a third reading beside points 5 and 6. Set the rule before the run.

**16. Centring can create or hide a shape.** In paper 22, jPCA found no circles on shuffled data until the across-condition mean stayed in. PC traces stayed curved after condition shuffling, because units kept one time order.

Inference: the role and question centring in point 3 is not neutral. Run the main test with and without it. One shared order, such as reply position, could also bend a cloud with no role structure.

**17. Compare stages with a stage-level null.** Paper 23 shuffles units between stages. It scores each stage against written generative models. Paper 25 scores alignment against a within-day split ceiling, and uses surrogates that should fail. Paper 25 also raises the worry that CCA is too powerful.

Inference: our units keep their index, so a paired test is possible. Permute checkpoint labels within cells. Split wordings to give a ceiling per checkpoint. Report the part of a stage change that a linear map removes, then the remainder. S6 covers the choice of metric. Do not assume a monotonic path, which also matches `LITERATURE.md`.

**18. Manifold capacity is not a shape test.** Paper 19 assumes randomly oriented, uncorrelated manifolds, and calls correlations further work. It shows ring dimension scaling like that of polytopes.

Inference: this confirms the row in section 4 from the primary source. Skip capacity as a curved-vs-flat test. It may still describe role separability.

**19. One manifold or several cannot be settled here.** Paper 18 poses two options for task planes: patches of one universal curved manifold, or distinct manifolds. It leaves this open.

Inference: a stage change in our cloud has the same two readings. State this in the wording limits.
