### A fourth meaning of "curved"

`LITERATURE.md` lists three meanings. S3 adds a fourth. Inference: it is unrelated to the other three.

| Meaning | Example | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Path curvature: turning angle along an ordered sequence | Hosseini, Hénaff 2019, Skean | Angle between successive steps |

The floor differs by source. Hosseini reports 120 degrees for a random path. Hénaff reports 90 degrees for random vectors in high dimension. Inference: the two nulls differ (independent points against independent steps), so any floor must name its null. In Hénaff the measure needs a given order; role cells have none.

## 6. Points that contradict or change our plan

Ordered by effect on the plan.

### A. Points against the hypothesis or the claim

**1. No directional prior exists for "more curved".** Hosseini finds that training lowers path curvature, with no fine-tuning test. Kaufman finds surface curvature rises at the last layer only, with a long flat plateau. Ansuini argues flattening is not general. Brahma reports flattening (abstract only). Hénaff 2019 finds object-recognition nets raise path curvature on natural videos. Skean finds path curvature falls in mid layers during Pythia training. Inference: the literature gives no reason to expect a rise, so a two-sided test fits better. These are also different objects (N6).

**2. "Manifold" cannot be the claim.** Schulte Lemma 3: token-based LLM states form a countable set, so true ID is zero. Lee App. C concedes the point. Inference: the lemma concerns the limit at zero scale and does not block finite-scale models. So the claim should read "a smooth nonlinear model predicts held-out cells better than flat and clump models". It should not read "the cloud forms a curved manifold".

**3. ID-type evidence may only restate the linear spectrum.** Schulte §4 finds ID estimates track von Neumann entropy, and rising norms drive neighbour distances. Lee §4.3 finds the opposite kind of result: shuffling collapses ID while raising PCA dimension. Lee App. I adds that linear dimension tracks gzip size while TwoNN does not. IdEst App. F finds MST dimension falls while RankMe rises during DINO training. Schulte's evidence is across layers of finished models, not across training, so the conflict is narrower than it first looked. None of these tests post-training. Inference: if we report any ID, also report F13 per checkpoint. RankMe and an ID trend may or may not be independent; that is an empirical check, not a given.

**4. Curvature and clusters are linked in the one direct measure.** Kaufman §5 places the highest curvature at class clusters. Mickus shows anisotropy and clusters go together. Inference: a stage that sharpens clumps could raise a curvature score with no continuous bend. This raises the value of the clump arm.

### B. Points that change the design

**5. Fixing one layer is risky.** ID has phases by depth (Valeriani, Cheng). Doimo Fig. 6 finds fine-tuning changes late layers most, and a transition near mid-depth. Liu reports the same band. Cheng notes OLMo-7B (v1, not OLMo-2) has a second peak as high as the first. Inference: if a stage shifts the transition layer, a fixed layer sees a false stage effect. Use at least one layer on each side of the transition, chosen without the outcome. `LITERATURE.md` says "fix layer and token first"; this qualifies it.

**6. A fixed text may hide the effect.** Ruppik §4.1: fine-tuning lowers local ID on its own data (effect 1.19), and hardly on other data (0.08, 0.1). This holds for Llama-3.1-8B too (App. C.1). Liu notes a fixed probe can understate specialisation. Inference: a null on one text is weak evidence. This turns the "second text source" row into a requirement. Record how close each text is to the SFT data.

**7. Expect the change at SFT, not a steady climb.** Wang: DPO from base keeps the chart (overlap 0.939, 7.64 degrees). SFT moves it (0.745, 25.07 degrees). DPO after SFT stays in the SFT geometry. Li 2025 also finds non-monotonic spectra. Wang covers two behaviours on Llama-3.1-8B with linear charts. **Caution from the full read:** the update sizes differ. SFT used learning rate 1e-5 for 100–600 steps. DPO used 5e-7 for 60 steps. Inference: "SFT moves the chart, DPO keeps it" may partly reflect a smaller update, not the objective. Wang's controlled runs do not rule this out in what I read. Inference: base to SFT is still the first contrast to examine, but do not predict a null at DPO or RL. The 10-checkpoint trend should not be a single slope.

**8. Stage is not objective.** Wang §5: public Base–Instruct pairs confound objective, data, duration and implementation. Inference: OLMo-2 stages have this confound, so report stage effects only.

**9. Add a fourth arm: a union of local pieces.** Brown supports disjoint pieces of different dimension. Bhalla (S4) warns local flat pieces can mimic a curve. Inference: flat vs clump vs curved omits the case of role-local subspaces. A per-role low-rank model fits this slot. It also needs Brown's random-partition control: the same model with shuffled role labels. Cohen's shuffled assignment is the same null.

**10. Clumps may be the main rival at later stages.** Doimo §4.3: SFT makes 4–5 large answer clusters, holding over 70% of the data in layers 20–30 of Llama3-8B. Wu and Papyan: collapse grows with scale and training, though only up to 205M parameters, on TinyStories, last layer, post-training out of scope. Doimo's clusters are answer letters, not roles. Inference: treat "clumps rise with stage" as a live competing result, not only a control.

**11. 40 cells per role is below every local method.** Needs: about 1024 neighbours (Kaufman), N exponential in d (Erba), ID under 15–20 for density (Doimo), 300–1000 per class for class ID (Brown fails at 500), P near 50 for capacity (Cohen). Chen shows naive high-dimensional curvature can land nowhere near the truth. Inference: local curvature is out of reach, so the global held-out comparison is the right type of test. Do not report a per-role curvature number. The word "curvature" in any result should mean predictive nonlinearity.

**12. Calibrate by simulation.** Chen §3.2 proposes simulating clouds near a flat subspace at matched density and noise (a proposal; §4 runs spheres only, so the earlier "§5 simulates" was wrong). Ansuini uses a covariance-matched Gaussian. Hénaff 2019 runs the full version: matched simulated observers, refit with the same method. This gives the surrogate row in `LITERATURE.md` a published source; it is no longer only inference. Inference: run the whole GPLVM pipeline on a matched flat surrogate and a matched clump surrogate per checkpoint, and report the false-win rate.

**13. The field's main control is out of scope for us.** Random init is used by Ansuini, Kaufman, Hosseini, Cohen, Cheng. Shuffled text by Cheng, Lee. Known-structure text by Lee. `LITERATURE.md` lists random init as needing a GPU. Lee App. I finds linear dimension tracks text complexity even at initialisation. Inference: without this control, a stage effect has no architecture floor. State 0 and text features are weaker. The report should say so, or a small GPU run should be priced.

**14. "Noise" means something else for us.** Noise papers treat it as measurement error (Valeriani, Ruppik, Chun). Cohen assumes none. Inference: a forward pass on fixed text is deterministic, so S7 has no trial noise here. The GPLVM noise term will absorb real role-by-question structure. Chun's two-trial correction has no direct analogue. A split over questions is the closest stand-in, but it is not a true repeat. Skean's text perturbations suggest a second stand-in: paraphrased questions, which need new forward passes. Chun warns that noise shared across trials is not removed, and a paraphrase shares much with its source. Noise differences between stages should be read as unexplained structure.

**15. Crossed cells break sampling assumptions.** Chun needs independent rows. TwoNN needs a local Poisson process. Inference: 20 selected roles crossed with 40 questions meet neither, so nearest neighbours of a cell will often share a role or question. Any neighbour-based number on this cloud reflects the design as much as the geometry. IdEst Fig. 2 shows the risk directly: evenly spaced points on a helix break TwoNN and MLE, with no noise at all. This is a further reason to prefer held-out prediction.

### B2. Points added by the second session

Ordered by effect on the plan. Points 16–18 change the design. The others refine it.

**16. Fit-then-measure is biased, and curvature is a positive number.** Hénaff: fitting a trajectory and then reading its curvature drifts toward 90 degrees at the real data size. A truly straight path still read about 29 degrees. Chen shows the same kind of bias for tangent estimates. Inference: this applies to any shape number read from fitted GPLVM latents or a fitted mean function. Point 12 gives false-win rates on flat and clump surrogates. It does not show the pipeline can see a real bend. Add a recovery curve: surrogates at several known bends, including zero, at 800 cells. Report detection and bias at each level. Without it, a null result has no stated power.

**17. Unit RMS does not deal with rogue dimensions.** Timkey: 1–5 dimensions can decide cosine and Euclidean distance, and removing them can change which pairs are close. Those dimensions tracked position 0 and the "." token, with little effect on output. Mean removal alone did not fix this; standardising each dimension did. Lee App. I saw the same problem break PCA in Pythia-160m. Limit: models up to GPT-2 size, and OLMo-2 is unchecked. Inference: an RBF kernel uses Euclidean distance, so a few dimensions could set the fit. A stage effect could be growth in those dimensions. Add F18 per checkpoint, and rerun the model comparison with per-dimension standardisation as a stated sensitivity arm. This qualifies the "unit RMS" row in `LITERATURE.md`.

**18. The role by question grid can test additivity directly.** `LITERATURE.md` already asks for an additivity check, from Sarfati. Cohen SI §1.1.4 gives a published form: two factors are factorised when the 2-d dimension is twice the 1-d dimension, and interact when it is more. Inference: the flat arm should include an additive role plus question model. A curved win over a plain low-rank model could be role by question interaction, not a bend. Interaction is a real finding, but it is not curvature.

**19. Role centres and within-role shape need separate nulls.** Cohen SI §1.1.5 replaces manifold centres with random vectors, and permutes neurons per manifold to remove axis alignment. Centres mattered much more than axes for capacity. Inference: keep each role's within-role cloud and randomise its centre, to ask whether a curved win comes from how roles sit or from their inner shape. This extends the random-partition control in point 9.

**20. Do not use primary CCA alone to compare checkpoints.** Wang: matched random coordinates give primary CCA of 0.667 and 0.817, so a high first correlation shows little. Mean CCA separates them (random 0.222 and 0.233; behaviour 0.515 and 0.648). Those are cross-model figures. Wang also projects later checkpoints into the base coordinates, which keeps one frame. Inference: for our paired cells, fit on one checkpoint and score on the next, in fixed coordinates, as well as fitting each one alone. That separates "the surface moved" from "the surface changed shape".

**21. Smooth or abrupt change depends on the index.** Doimo 2020 App. A.4: linear CKA rises early, while neighbour overlap jumps late. Gaussian CKA moves between them with bandwidth. Inference: a smooth stage trend in CKA and a jump in neighbours may be one event. In a GP, the kernel length scale may play the same part, so report it per checkpoint.

**22. Direct evidence on ID across instruction tuning disagrees.** Yin §5.3: on Llama-2-7B, local ID rises over 3,000 steps, measured on TriviaQA and TydiQA rather than the tuning data, with dips that match accuracy dips. Ruppik §4.1: ID falls on the tuning data and hardly moves elsewhere. Skean: fine-tuning moves prompt entropy either way, by method. Settings differ (different estimators, models and data). Inference: this again supports a two-sided test. It also adds a rival: if persona replies mix sources, N16 predicts higher ID with no bend.

### Open questions

- No paper in S3 measures cloud curvature across SFT, DPO and RL. Ruppik names RL tuning as future work only.
- The Schulte and Lee conflict on linear vs nonlinear dimension is narrower but unresolved. No paper tests it across post-training.
- Cai 2021 and Brahma 2016 are at abstract level only, so points that rest on them are second-hand.
- Is the SFT vs DPO geometry gap in Wang an objective effect or an update-size effect? No run in what I read matches update size.
- Do rogue dimensions exist in OLMo-2, and do they change across stages? No paper here covers this. It can be checked on our own cells.
- Liu 2026 is weak evidence. Do not lean on it alone.
